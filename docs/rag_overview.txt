Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that enhances large language models (LLMs) by retrieving relevant information from an external knowledge base before generating a response.

Instead of relying solely on what the model learned during training, RAG dynamically pulls in up-to-date, domain-specific documents at query time and provides them as context to the LLM.

Key advantages of RAG:
- Keeps answers grounded in real, verifiable sources.
- Allows the model to cite specific documents or URLs.
- Reduces hallucination by constraining the answer to retrieved content.
- Does not require expensive model retraining when knowledge changes.
- Works well with private, internal enterprise data.

RAG is commonly used in enterprise applications where accuracy, traceability, and up-to-date information are more important than raw model creativity.
